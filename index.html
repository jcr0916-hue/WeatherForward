Read the map.
Trust your own forecast.

That map on the evening news isn't a mystery — it's a story about what the sky will do next. We're building Weather Forward to help you read it, and to make your own forecast with real confidence.

Follow the build See the plan

Knowing what "a 40% chance of rain" means isn't the point. Knowing what you'd forecast is.

Most weather content either dumps concepts with no application, or just tells you the answer. Weather Forward is designed to fill the gap between the two — turning you from a passive viewer of forecasts into someone who can glance at a map and form their own read.

The plan: learn on real-inspired scenarios, then apply it to live weather.

The learning loop we're building

Three steps, repeated until the map starts making sense.

Step 1

Decode

You'll take a real-looking weather map — fronts, pressure, radar — and break it down piece by piece in plain language.

Step 2

Predict

Given what you see, what happens next? You'll decide where the rain goes and what the temperature does.

Step 3

Build confidence

You'll see what actually happened, and why your read was right or wrong. Every round will sharpen your eye.

Two stages, one skill

Stage 1 — Crafted · Planned

Scenarios inspired by real weather

Controlled examples built from genuine events, so they feel authentic while stripping away the noise. Each one will isolate a single skill — temperature, then rain, then wind and storms — to build your foundation.

Stage 2 — Live · Planned

Real weather, real forecasts

Pick a city, read today's actual conditions, and make a forecast you'll check against the real sky. This is where forecasting becomes a practice of confidence rather than certainty.
