Knowing what "a 40% chance of rain" means isn't the point. Knowing what you'd forecast is.

Most weather content either dumps concepts with no application, or just tells you the answer. Weather Forward fills the gap between the two — it turns you from a passive viewer of forecasts into someone who can glance at a map and form their own read.

Learn on real-inspired scenarios, then apply it to live weather.

The Weather Forward loop

Three steps. Repeat until the map starts making sense.

Step 1

Decode

Take a real-looking weather map — fronts, pressure, radar — and break it down piece by piece in plain language.

Step 2

Predict

Given what you see, what happens next? Where does the rain go? What does the temperature do?

Step 3

Build confidence

See what actually happened, and why your read was right or wrong. Every round sharpens your eye.

Two ways in, one skill out

Stage 1 — Crafted

Scenarios inspired by real weather

Controlled examples built from genuine events, so they feel authentic but strip away the noise. Isolate one skill at a time — temperature, then rain, then wind and storms — and build your foundation.

Stage 2 — Live

Real weather, real forecasts

Pick a city, read today's actual conditions, and make a forecast you'll check against the real sky. This is where you learn that forecasting is about confidence, not certainty.
